1. Funnel Architecture — Authority Ladder → Funnel Stages

The funnel is the Authority Ladder made operational. The core motion is simple and repeats forever:

Educational content → profile visit → free resource or W0 pilot → W1/W2 paid booking → W5 retainer → referral.

The single most important rule, inherited from the package strategy: never sell above the rung the audience is on. Months 1–2 (September–October 2026) sell nothing — they teach. The first paid conversations happen only after pilots have run.

Leak-plugging principle: at every stage there is exactly one next step, always smaller than a sale. A post never asks for a booking; it asks for a comment or a download. A download never asks for money; it opens a conversation. A conversation asks only for a free W0. The W0 earns the right to propose W1. This is slow by design — from ~4 connections, trust is the entire asset.

2. Segment Playbooks

Five segments, five playbooks. The WHO business-case line is the only place statistics appear in outbound messages — one line, sourced, never embellished.

2.1 HR professionals & people managers (Lebanon) — the buyers

  • The pain, in their words: "Everyone is stretched. People are quietly exhausted, good staff leave without warning, and when I raise wellbeing the answer is 'we have no budget.' I need something practical I can actually run — not a poster campaign."
  • WHO-grounded business case line: "The WHO estimates around 12 billion working days are lost every year to depression and anxiety — roughly US$1 trillion in lost productivity — and its recommended workplace actions are exactly what a workshop can deliver: manager training and mental health literacy for staff."
  • Entry offer: free W0 lunch & learn (45–60 min) for one team, plus the DESK one-pager they can circulate internally.
  • Channel: LinkedIn DM after engagement (§5.1), then email with the one-pager PDF.
  • Proof they need: a structured agenda they can show their leadership, the non-clinical boundary stated plainly (this de-risks it for them), and evidence Dorina understands office reality — her 12+ years in administration is the proof. After the first pilots: a one-line summary of what a previous session covered (no client names without permission).

2.2 SME owners (Lebanon) — the business-case segment

  • The pain, in their words: "I can't afford turnover and I can't afford sick days. But I also can't afford a big consultancy programme. If I spend a day of my team's time, I need it to be useful the same week."
  • WHO business case line: "Almost 60% of the world's population is in work, and the WHO names excessive workload, long hours, and lack of control as key workplace risks — the exact things a half-day of practical structure addresses."
  • Entry offer: W0 for the owner plus their team leads, framed as "one lunch hour, zero cost, you judge the value yourself."
  • Channel: warm introduction where possible (owners buy on trust); otherwise LinkedIn DM referencing a specific post they engaged with; coworking-space partner events (§8).
  • Proof they need: price certainty (a clear range up front), minimal time cost, hybrid/low-tech delivery options (electricity and connectivity constraints are real — Dorina should always offer a projector-free, printout-based version), and a same-day takeaway (the DESK worksheet).

2.3 Administrative professionals / EAs / office managers — the tribe

  • The pain, in their words: "I hold everyone else's calendar, everyone else's stress, and everyone else's deadlines. Nobody asks how I'm managing mine. Saying no feels impossible in my role."
  • WHO business case line (peer framing, softer): "The WHO lists workload, pace, and lack of control over one's work among the main workplace risks to mental health — a description of the average admin desk."
  • Entry offer: free content first (this segment is community, not revenue): the DESK one-pager, plus early invitations to the free WMHD open online session. Paid path is W3 — pitched to their employers, often via the tribe member championing it internally.
  • Channel: LinkedIn content and comments (Dorina is one of them — peer voice, not vendor voice), plus DMs that never sell.
  • Proof they need: authenticity. Dorina's own story — 12+ years at the admin desk, including a law-firm environment — is the entire proof. This segment converts to advocates who bring her into their companies.

2.4 nonprofit/humanitarian organization / development-sector staff — the mission-aligned segment

  • The pain, in their words: "Our teams carry heavy work in a heavy context. We talk about staff care in every proposal, but the actual budget line is small and the time is smaller."
  • WHO business case line: "The WHO frames mental health as a basic human right and recommends workplace mental health literacy and stress-management skills as core interventions — a natural fit for organisations already committed to duty of care."
  • Entry offer: W0 or a discounted W2 for managers, framed as staff-care programming; W4 online for field-dispersed teams.
  • Channel: email (this sector lives in email), referencing shared values and the national conversation the MoPH's National Mental Health Programme has opened around workplace mental health — always as ecosystem context, never as claimed affiliation.
  • Proof they need: boundary clarity above all (nonprofit/humanitarian organizations are rightly careful about anyone near "mental health" without clinical credentials — the non-clinical educator framing, stated first, is what opens the door), Arabic/English bilingual delivery, and referral-pathway literacy (the "S — Support" pillar of DESK: help-seeking as a skill, signposting to qualified professionals).

2.5 Diaspora & GCC-based professionals / remote teams — the online segment

  • The pain, in their words: "I work across time zones, my team is scattered, and 'wellbeing' at my company is a webinar nobody attends. I want something short, live, and actually built for remote work."
  • WHO business case line: "The WHO names long or inflexible hours and lack of control among key workplace risks — remote work amplifies both, which is why the session focuses on boundaries and routine design."
  • Entry offer: the free WMHD open online session (§6) as the taster; W4 (90-minute online) as the paid step.
  • Channel: LinkedIn only (content travels borderlessly); posts tagged to remote-work themes; diaspora networks and Lebanese professional groups online.
  • Proof they need: a smooth online experience (test-run tech, recordings where agreed, materials delivered digitally), scheduling flexibility across time zones, and USD invoicing simplicity.

4. Landing Page / One-Pager Copy (Full Copy — Doubles as PDF for Email)

Use as a simple one-page site (e.g., a lightweight page builder) and as a designed PDF attached to outreach emails. Copy below is final draft for Dorina's approval.

Practical mental health skills for real workplaces.

Workshops that teach your team to manage stress, workload, and boundaries — in plain language, in Arabic or English, with tools they'll use the same week.

The problem, honestly stated. The World Health Organization estimates that around 12 billion working days are lost every year to depression and anxiety — about US$1 trillion in lost productivity. And the risks it names are ordinary office realities: excessive workloads and pace, understaffing, long or inflexible hours, lack of control over one's work, under-used skills, and workplace culture that makes it hard to speak up. None of this is exotic. All of it is manageable — if people are taught how.

The approach: the DESK Method (proposed). A four-part framework for practical mental health management at work — built at an admin desk, not in a lecture hall:

  • D — Detect. Stress literacy: recognising your early signals before they become your normal.
  • E — Establish. Boundaries, routines, and workload visibility — structure that protects energy.
  • S — Support. Help-seeking as a professional skill, and knowing the referral pathways when someone needs more than a workshop.
  • K — Keep. Sustaining the habits: weekly reviews, real rest, leisure and physical activity.

What a workshop includes. Every engagement starts with a pre-call so the session fits your team, not a template. You get: a live, interactive session (in person, online, or hybrid — including a low-tech, printout-based option for venues where power or connectivity is unreliable), a DESK one-pager for every attendee, and a one-page summary report for HR within a week. Formats run from a free 45-minute lunch & learn taster to a half-day flagship, a 2-hour manager session, and quarterly refresher programmes.

Who's teaching. Dorina Salman spent 12+ years as an executive assistant and administrator — including in a law-firm environment — holding calendars, deadlines, and other people's pressure. She teaches workplace wellbeing from lived office experience, in Arabic and English. She is not a therapist, and she'll be the first to tell you so. These are educational workshops in stress literacy, boundaries, and workload structure — and part of what they teach is when and how to reach a qualified professional.

Educational content on workplace wellbeing — not therapy, diagnosis, or crisis support. If you're struggling, please reach a qualified professional; in an emergency, contact local emergency services.

FAQ

  1. Is this therapy? No — and that's a feature, not a disclaimer. These are educational workshops: skills, structure, and literacy. Dorina does not diagnose, treat, or counsel. One module (DESK's "Support" pillar) specifically teaches teams how to seek qualified professional help when it's needed.
  2. Do you do this in Arabic? Yes. Sessions run in Arabic, English, or a natural mix (المزيج اللبناني الطبيعي — the natural Lebanese blend), whatever suits your team. All handouts are available in both languages.
  3. Will you tell us if someone on the team is struggling? No. Sessions are educational and group-based; nobody is assessed, and nothing personal shared in a session is reported. If an individual needs support, the session equips them — and their manager — to find qualified help.
  4. What does it cost? The lunch & learn taster is free for qualifying teams. Paid formats range from a 90-minute online session to a half-day flagship — ask for the current rate card, and expect a straight answer.
  5. Our power and internet are unreliable — can this still work? Yes. Every format has a hybrid or fully low-tech version: printed materials, no projector required. This is designed for Lebanon.
  6. Can you prove it will reduce sick days or anxiety? No — and be wary of anyone who says yes. What a workshop reliably delivers is what the WHO recommends workplaces provide: mental health literacy, stress-management skills, and manager awareness. Skills, not medical outcomes.

5.8 Objection handling — the six objections Dorina will hear, and the honest responses

These are conversational responses, not scripts to recite. The pattern is always the same: agree with the legitimate part, reframe with one fact or one boundary, offer the smallest next step.

Objection Response
"We have no budget for wellbeing." "Completely fair — most Lebanese SMEs don't have a wellbeing line. That's exactly why the taster is free: you spend one lunch hour, not one dollar, and judge for yourself whether the paid formats earn a place in next quarter's budget. And when we do talk numbers, they're SME numbers, not consultancy numbers."
"Our people are too busy for this." "That's usually the strongest sign it's needed — the WHO lists excessive workload and pace among the main workplace risks to mental health. The taster is 45 minutes over lunch, and the half-day format is designed to pay back its time in workload structure the same week. But I'd never push a session onto a team in crisis-delivery mode; let's pick a calmer window."
"Isn't this a therapist's job?" "A therapist's job is treatment — and I don't touch that; I'm an educator, not a clinician, and I say so on every slide. My job is the layer before: literacy, boundaries, workload structure, and teaching people how and when to reach a qualified professional. Those are workplace skills, and the WHO specifically recommends workplaces build them."
"We tried a wellbeing talk before; nothing changed." "One-off talks usually don't change anything — I agree. That's why every session ends with specific habits, a one-page tool, and a follow-up sequence, and why the format I most recommend long-term is the quarterly refresher, not the single event. Skills stick with repetition, not applause."
"Can you guarantee results — less stress, fewer sick days?" "No, and I'd be careful with anyone who guarantees health outcomes. What I guarantee is the education: your team will leave with a shared language for stress, concrete boundary and workload tools, and knowledge of where real help lives. What you do with those skills is where results come from."
"Why you? You were an administrator." "That's exactly why. I spent 12+ years at the desk where everyone else's pressure lands — including in a law firm. I teach what I had to learn to survive it, in the language of people who actually sit in these offices, in Arabic or English. I'm not translating a textbook; I'm handing over a working system."

7. Seasonal Campaign Modules

Each module aligns with the corresponding month in 12_MONTH_LINKEDIN_CONTENT_CALENDAR.md; the funnel role of each is defined here.

7.1 October — World Mental Health Day flagship (Oct 2026, repeats Oct 2027)

  • Goal: maximum visibility + fill the W0 pipeline (10 offers out; 4–6 booked).
  • Hook: "October 10 is World Mental Health Day — and the workplace is where most adults spend most of their waking hours. This week, one practical skill a day." (WMHD's past themes include workplace mental health — the alignment is natural, not forced.)
  • Assets needed: 5-post series (one per DESK letter + a capstone), open-session slide deck + low-bandwidth fallback plan, registration link, DESK one-pager (EN/AR), W0 offer DM, 10-company target list.
  • Offer attached: free open online session (public); W0 pilots (private, targeted).
  • KPI: open-session registrations and attendance; W0 offers accepted; profile-visit spike week-over-week.

7.2 January — "Reset, Done Right" (Jan 2027)

  • Goal: convert year-start motivation into W1 bookings; re-engage December's warm pipeline.
  • Hook: "New year's resolutions fail because they're goals without structure. A reset that lasts is a routine, not a promise. Here's how to build one at work." (Anti-hype framing — no transformation promises.)
  • Assets needed: 3–4 post mini-series on routines and workload structure (DESK "E" and "K"), a simple "January workload reset" worksheet as the month's free download, §5.5 proposal ready with January/February dates.
  • Offer attached: W1 flagship, positioned as the team's structured start-of-year session; W2 for managers planning the year.
  • KPI: worksheet downloads; proposals sent; W1 bookings for Q1.

7.3 Ramadan-aware campaign (around February–February–March 2027 — dates shift by moon sighting; never state exact dates)

  • Goal: trust and cultural credibility; retention touchpoint for W5 clients; light W4 pipeline.
  • Hook: "Ramadan changes the shape of the workday — energy, hours, routines. That's not a problem to push through; it's a rhythm to plan for, with compassion for yourself and your colleagues." Framing is energy, routines, and compassion — inclusive of fasting and non-fasting colleagues alike, never prescriptive about observance.
  • Assets needed: 2–3 posts on routine adaptation and considerate scheduling; a short "Ramadan workday rhythm" one-pager (EN/AR); a W5-client refresher session variant on adjusted routines.
  • Offer attached: nothing sold publicly during the month — this campaign is deliberately generous. Privately: the W5 quarterly refresher lands here for retainer clients; W4 offered to remote teams wanting a short seasonal session.
  • KPI: engagement quality (comments, shares, DMs), W5 refreshers delivered, inbound enquiries in the following month.

Hygiene rules: every row has a next action and a date, or it moves to "Closed–not now" with a revisit month; "Closed–not now" is a parking lot, not a graveyard — WMHD and January triggers sweep it; nothing lives only in LinkedIn DMs — if it matters, it's a row.

10.3 Weekly 30-minute marketing review ritual (fixed slot, e.g., Friday)

  1. Minutes 0–5: last week's numbers into the tracker (posts published, impressions, new conversations, downloads).
  2. Minutes 5–15: walk every open pipeline row; execute or reschedule each overdue next action. No row skipped.
  3. Minutes 15–20: reply debt — clear every unanswered comment and DM.
  4. Minutes 20–25: next week — confirm the 3 scheduled posts (per approval workflow), pick the week's single outreach priority (never more than one campaign push at a time).
  5. Minutes 25–30: one-line journal — what worked, what didn't, one adjustment. Monthly, these lines become the review input for 12_MONTH_LINKEDIN_CONTENT_CALENDAR.md adjustments.

This ritual is itself DESK's "Keep" pillar applied to marketing: a weekly review that sustains the system. If Dorina runs nothing else in a chaotic week, she runs this half hour.
