# Dorina Women in Power Circle — Master Implementation Plan

## 1. Strategy in one sentence

Dorina Salman builds a recognisable women’s wellbeing practice in Lebanon by teaching one practical signature sequence—the CIRCLE Method—consistently from LinkedIn content to inquiry, delivery, seven-day follow-up, feedback, and the next repeatable program.

This is a **Dorina-led brand and service system**. Nadine may support a specific session when useful and after her exact role is confirmed; the public identity, method, participant relationship, and follow-up remain with Dorina.

## 3. Audience and customer jobs

### Primary audience

Women in Lebanon who carry recurring pressure at work, home, during caregiving, or through life transitions and want practical language, a calm structure, and tools they can repeat.

### The jobs they are trying to do

- recognise stress earlier instead of only after overload;
- describe an emotion without needing a long personal explanation;
- understand one recurring trigger pattern;
- respond with a clear boundary or request;
- know who to ask for support and what to ask for;
- leave with a realistic plan for a difficult day;
- find a small, respectful group or a more private starting format.

## 5. Offer architecture

### A. First Women in Power Circle

- **Purpose:** introduce the full CIRCLE Method in a small women-only setting.
- **Recommended pilot:** 12–18 women, approximately three hours.
- **Experience:** reflection, optional sharing, breath reset, gentle movement, workbook practice, phrase rehearsal, and closing plan.
- **Take-home set:** one Trigger Map, one boundary phrase, one Support Map, one Bad-Day Card, and the seven-day sequence.
- **Use as:** the first proof of usefulness and operational readiness.

### B. Private wellbeing consultation

- **Purpose:** apply the CIRCLE sequence to one recurring daily pressure point in a more private setting.
- **Experience:** identify the moment, complete one Trigger Map, choose one boundary/routine/support step, and define a seven-day practice.
- **Take-home set:** one-page personal CIRCLE summary.
- **Scope:** practical wellbeing education and coaching; keep deeper needs on an appropriate qualified-support pathway.

### C. Hosted circle

- **Purpose:** deliver the method for a women’s community, studio, organization, or workplace.
- **Experience:** tailor examples and discussion prompts to one agreed theme while preserving the same six moves and outputs.
- **Host brief:** audience, language, access needs, room format, privacy expectations, timing, and follow-up owner.
- **Rule:** the host can shape context, not expand Dorina’s public claims or change the method into an untested promise.

### D. Four-week program, after the pilot

Build only after one pilot has been reviewed.

1. Week 1 — body signals, breath, and emotion language;
2. Week 2 — Trigger Map and repeated patterns;
3. Week 3 — boundary phrases, requests, and support;
4. Week 4 — Bad-Day Card, integration, and next-step plan.

Do not launch an eight-week version until the four-week version has stable delivery, useful feedback, and repeat demand.

## 6. Dorina’s signature CIRCLE Method

The method must appear in the same order on the landing page, LinkedIn, facilitator guide, participant workbook, consultation summary, and follow-up.

| Move | Session technique | Dorina’s prompt | Participant output | Follow-up connection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C — Come back to the body** | 90-second Body Signal Scan, comfortable Breath Reset, optional breath-led movement | “Where do you notice the first signal?” | One recognisable early body cue | Day 1 body cue; Day 2 breath reset |
| **I — Identify the emotion** | Emotion naming and need prompt | “I notice ___, and I may need ___.” | One precise present-moment sentence | Day 3 emotion-and-need sentence |
| **R — Read the trigger** | Five-part Trigger Map | “What happened, what did the body signal, what emotion appeared, what was needed, and what followed?” | One completed map for a recurring moment | Day 4 Trigger Map |
| **C — Choose the response** | Breath pause, response menu, Boundary Phrase rehearsal | “What is the smallest useful response available now?” | One rehearsed line and one next action | Day 5 boundary phrase |
| **L — Link to support** | Three-ring Support Map | “Who can listen, who can help practically, what will you ask, and when?” | Named support plus exact asks | Day 6 support request |
| **E — Embed the practice** | Bad-Day Card and seven-day integration | “What will make this visible and repeatable on a real day?” | Three reset tools, two people, one next step | Day 7 review and adjustment |

### Why the method is distinctive

- It starts with observable signals instead of abstract advice.
- It makes every insight produce a physical or written output.
- It keeps personal disclosure optional.
- It connects self-awareness to boundaries and support, not awareness alone.
- It continues for seven days instead of ending when the event ends.
- It gives Dorina a consistent teaching language across all formats and channels.

## 7. Technique protocols

### 7.1 Body Signal Scan

Guide attention through jaw, shoulders, chest, stomach, hands, and feet. Participants notice tension, temperature, pace, pressure, or restlessness without scoring or interpreting it. Each person chooses one early signal she can recognise later.

### 7.2 Breath Reset

Use a comfortable counted breath with an unforced, slightly longer exhale. Use it on arrival, before a difficult reflection, and before choosing a response. Never frame a specific count as mandatory; comfort and choice come first.

### 7.3 Gentle breath-led movement

Offer seated and standing versions of simple shoulder, spine, hand, and foot movements. Keep range comfortable, intensity low, and participation optional. If Nadine supports this segment, confirm her exact role before naming it publicly.

### 7.4 Emotion naming

Move from broad language (“bad”, “too much”, “fine”) to one more precise emotion word. Pair the word with the stem: “I notice ___, and I may need ___.” Participants can write rather than speak.

### 7.5 Trigger Map

Use five boxes in this order:

1. situation — the observable moment;
2. body signal — what appeared first;
3. emotion — one or two precise words;
4. need — time, clarity, space, rest, information, practical help, or support;
5. usual response — what typically happens next.

Then add a sixth review prompt: “What is one different response I could try?”

### 7.6 Boundary Phrase Lab

Practise concise phrases aloud or in writing:

- “I need time before I answer.”
- “I cannot take this on today.”
- “What I can offer is…”
- “I can continue this conversation when…”
- “I need support with…”

The output is one sentence the participant is willing to use—not a long script.

### 7.7 Support Map

Use three rings:

- **everyday listening:** trusted people who can listen without fixing;
- **practical help:** people or services for transport, childcare, work, logistics, or information;
- **qualified support:** verified options for needs outside the circle’s scope.

For each selected person or service, write the exact ask and the moment to use it.

### 7.8 Bad-Day Card

One private card containing:

- three reset tools that genuinely fit the participant;
- two people or resources;
- one smallest useful next step;
- one reminder that she can pause or ask for help.

### 7.9 Seven-day integration

Track usefulness, difficulty, and adjustment—not perfect completion or health outcomes.

### Facilitation rules

- Dorina opens, teaches the method, manages transitions, and closes.
- Sharing is invited, never required.
- Participants may write, observe, adapt, or pass.
- No participant advises or interprets another participant.
- Movement always has seated/standing and opt-out choices.
- Private stories are not used in marketing.
- Any issue outside the agreed practical-wellbeing scope moves to the prepared referral protocol.

## 9. Seven-day follow-up sequence

Keep every message short enough for WhatsApp or email.

1. **Day 1 — Notice:** Where did your first body cue appear today?
2. **Day 2 — Breathe:** Try the Breath Reset once before a routine answer. What changed, if anything?
3. **Day 3 — Name:** Complete: “I notice ___, and I may need ___.”
4. **Day 4 — Read:** Put one recurring moment into the five Trigger Map boxes.
5. **Day 5 — Choose:** Use or rehearse one boundary phrase.
6. **Day 6 — Link:** Make one specific support request from your map.
7. **Day 7 — Embed:** Review your Bad-Day Card. Keep what helped and adjust what did not fit.

Final message: invite private feedback, ask whether the participant wants the next-circle announcement, and offer the appropriate next step without pressure.

## 10. Feedback, testimonial, and learning loop

### Immediate feedback questions

1. Which tool was clearest?
2. Which tool are you most likely to use this week?
3. What felt confusing, rushed, or unnecessary?
4. Did the pace and level of participation feel respectful?
5. Which format would interest you next: another circle, a short program, a private consultation, or none for now?

### Seven-day feedback questions

1. Which tool did you actually try?
2. What made it easy or difficult to repeat?
3. What would make the follow-up more useful?

### Public words rule

Ask separately for permission to use a specific experience quote. Store the approved wording and channel. Do not turn private details into content, imply results beyond the feedback, or write a quote on someone’s behalf.

### Authority loop

After each circle, Dorina records:

- the three questions participants asked most often;
- the two techniques they understood most easily;
- the one point that needs clearer teaching;
- the next LinkedIn post inspired by an anonymised recurring question;
- the one delivery change for the next circle.

This turns delivery into better content and better content into better-qualified inquiries.
