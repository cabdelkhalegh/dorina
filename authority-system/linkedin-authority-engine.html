1.2 About section (full draft)

Twelve years behind the executive desk taught me something no report ever could: workplaces rarely break people loudly. They break people quietly — one skipped lunch, one 7 pm "quick favor," one blurred boundary at a time.

They also taught me the reverse. The right systems protect people quietly, too: a guarded calendar, a visible workload, a team that can name stress without shame.

That's the work I do now. I'm a workplace wellbeing educator and mental health literacy advocate. I translate guidance from authorities like the World Health Organization into desk-level habits, routines, and boundaries that working people in Lebanon can actually use.

The need is not a feeling — it's documented. The WHO estimates that 12 billion working days are lost each year to depression and anxiety, costing US$1 trillion in productivity, and that 15% of working-age adults were living with a mental disorder in 2019. The WHO also says effective workplace actions exist — including mental health literacy, awareness, and stress-management skills. That is exactly what I teach.

What I offer: • Workshops and lunch-and-learns on burnout prevention, stress literacy, and emotional organization (Arabic / English) • The DESK Method (proposed) — Detect, Establish, Support, Keep: wellbeing built like a system, because I've spent a career building systems • Talks and internal campaigns for teams, aligned with the spirit of Lebanon's national workplace mental health efforts

What I don't do — on purpose: I'm not a therapist, psychologist, or counselor, and my work is not therapy, diagnosis, or crisis support. It's education and prevention. When someone needs professional care, I help point them to qualified pathways — that's what responsible wellbeing work looks like.

📩 If you lead a team in Lebanon and want a practical, human, evidence-informed wellbeing session, send me a message with the word WORKSHOP.

(Statistics above cite the WHO Mental health at work fact sheet; keep them attributed exactly this way.)

3. The posting system

3.1 Cadence & formats

  • 3 posts/week — Monday, Wednesday, Friday (mornings, Beirut time; adjust with analytics after month 2).
  • Format rotation across the four pillars (defined in POSITIONING_AND_BRAND_ARCHITECTURE.md §7): text post with strong hook • carousel (systems/how-to) • quote/stat card (WHO-attributed) • story post • occasional poll • monthly long-form article from month 4.
  • Full month-by-month plan, themes, and written sample posts: 12_MONTH_LINKEDIN_CONTENT_CALENDAR.md.

3.2 Anatomy of an authority post (house style)

  1. Hook line (first 2 lines decide everything): a tension, a number, or a quiet truth. "Your best employee is not fine. She's organized."
  2. Body: short lines, white space, one idea per post; systems shown as steps; statistics always attributed to WHO by name.
  3. Boundary-safe framing: education, never instruction to ignore professional help; no diagnosis language.
  4. Close: one question to the reader (comments fuel reach) or one soft CTA (max 1 per week: "DM me WORKSHOP").
  5. Signature + hashtags (3–5): #WorkplaceWellbeing #MentalHealthLiteracy #BurnoutPrevention #Lebanon + one post-specific.
  6. Arabic variant where the topic is stigma/advocacy-heavy (pillar 3 lands hardest in Arabic).
