Dear Dorina — A Summary of Your Authority Strategy

Welcome to your strategy package. Before you open the bigger documents, this note explains — in plain language — what we built for you, why it is shaped the way it is, and what we need from you to make it fully yours.

The big idea

You asked for a path to becoming an authority in mental health management. We built you one — and we built it on the strongest possible foundation: honesty about what you offer.

Here is the truth that makes this strategy work rather than break: you are not a therapist, and you never need to pretend to be one. The most valuable, most missing voice in workplace mental health is not another clinician — it is the person who understands how work actually works. You have spent more than a decade inside demanding professional environments as an executive administrator. You know deadlines, difficult inboxes, invisible labor, pressure that never announces itself, and the systems that either protect people or quietly grind them down.

That is your authority. The strategy positions you as a Workplace Wellbeing Educator and Mental Health Literacy Advocate — someone who translates guidance from bodies like the World Health Organization into practical systems, routines, boundaries, and habits that working people in Lebanon can actually use.

And the need is enormous and documented. According to the WHO, an estimated 12 billion working days are lost every year to depression and anxiety, at a cost of about US$1 trillion in lost productivity, and 15% of working-age adults were estimated to live with a mental disorder in 2019. The WHO's recommended workplace responses include exactly what this plan trains you to deliver: mental health literacy and awareness to reduce stigma, and practical stress-management skills. You would be doing recognized, recommended work — not improvising.

Why the positioning is deliberately non-clinical

Because no clinical license is part of your profile, everything in this package draws one bright line:

You educate. You do not diagnose, treat, counsel, or handle crises. Anyone in distress is warmly referred to qualified professionals and emergency services.

This line is your greatest asset. It protects the people you serve, it reduces legal/reputational risk when reviewed with local counsel, and it makes you more bookable, not less — companies and HR teams can invite an educator without the liability questions a clinical claim would raise. Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health runs a National Mental Health Programme with workplace campaigns and anti-stigma materials; your work aligns with and points toward that national ecosystem instead of competing with it.

Every document you'll read repeats this boundary. Please never let anyone — including an enthusiastic client — pull you across it.

What's in the package

  1. A full strategic proposal — your 12-month roadmap from a quiet LinkedIn profile to a recognized regional voice, with phases, milestones, and revenue paths (paid corporate workshops, partnerships, and eventually digital products).
  2. Positioning and brand architecture — your title options, your story, your audience, and your signature framework: the DESK Method (proposed) (Detect, Establish, Support, Keep) — a proposed, brandable, non-clinical framework that turns your administrative superpower into your wellbeing teaching method.
  3. A LinkedIn authority engine — a complete profile rewrite (headline, About, experience, featured), a connection-building strategy that respects your starting point of ~4 connections, and a posting system.
  4. A 12-month content calendar — three posts a week, monthly themes, seasonal Lebanese hooks, World Mental Health Day (October 10) as your annual flagship moment, sample posts already written, and KPIs.
  5. A complete workshop system — a portfolio of four non-clinical workshops plus a lunch-and-learn format, with facilitator guides, exercises, handouts, opening and closing scripts, safety language, and follow-up sequences.
  6. Marketing funnel and campaigns — offers, landing page copy, outreach scripts, a launch plan, and partnership targets.
  7. A Lebanon opportunity map — where the demand is, by sector and season.
  8. An approval-first automation workflow — AI drafts content and graphics; nothing is ever posted until you have approved both the words and the visual. You stay in full control of your name and voice.
  9. A premium prompt library — ready-made AI prompts for every asset in this system, so future content can be generated quickly without losing your voice or your boundaries.
  10. Risk, ethics, and referral protocol — the exact disclaimer language, the prohibited claims list, and what to do if someone discloses distress in a workshop or in your DMs.

How your life changes, practically

  • Weeks 1–2: You update your LinkedIn profile using the rewrite we prepared, and quietly begin connecting with HR professionals, office managers, and colleagues in Lebanon.
  • Months 1–3: You post three times a week (drafts are prepared for your approval — expect ~30 minutes of review time per week). You start being seen.
  • Months 3–6: You deliver your first free pilot workshops to build testimonials, then your first paid sessions.
  • Months 6–12: You raise rates, build partnerships, anchor your year around World Mental Health Day in October, and open the Instagram extension when LinkedIn is running smoothly.

What we still need from you

The plan works today, but these answers will sharpen it. Nothing here blocks the start.

  1. Current role: Your LinkedIn shows Executive Assistant at Sami Nahas Law Firm (Feb 2014 – present), and separately we understand you may now be an Administrator at TiKiT. Which is current? Exact titles and dates so your profile is factually clean.
  2. Third language: Your profile implies a third language beyond Arabic and English — which one, and at what level?
  3. Your "why": Is there a personal (comfortable-to-share) reason workplace wellbeing matters to you? Your story is the heart of your About section — we drafted a version, but your truth will beat our draft.
  4. Training appetite: Would you complete a recognized non-clinical certificate (e.g., psychological first aid or mental health awareness training offered within the WHO/national programme ecosystem)? It strengthens credibility while staying safely non-clinical.
  5. Delivery language: Workshops in Arabic, English, or bilingual? Our materials assume bilingual with Arabic-first slides for local audiences.
  6. Availability & format: Hours per week you can commit; comfort with in-person vs. online delivery; access to a venue through your employer or network.
  7. Photography & visuals: A professional headshot and 2–3 workplace photos; brand color preferences.
  8. Approval channel: WhatsApp, email, or a shared document for approving weekly content drafts? (See the approval workflow document.)
  9. Employer alignment: Does your current employer know and support this personal brand? Any conflict-of-interest constraints we should design around?
  10. Budget: Roughly what can you invest monthly (design tools, scheduling tool, possible certificate course)? The plan includes a zero-budget path, but knowing helps.

One promise before you begin

Everything in this package was built from four authoritative sources — two WHO fact sheets, the WHO World Mental Health Day campaign page, and Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health National Mental Health Programme — plus your own visible profile facts. Nothing clinical was invented, and no statistic appears anywhere in these documents that doesn't trace back to those sources. When you speak from this material, you can do so with a straight back.

We're proud of this plan. More importantly, it is safe to be proud of — for you and for every person who will sit in your workshops.

Warmly, Your strategy team
