Strategic Proposal: Building Dorina Salman's Authority in Mental Health Management

Prepared for: Dorina Salman — Lebanon Scope: 12-month authority-building strategy, non-clinical positioning Status: Ready for Dorina's review and approval

1. Executive summary

This proposal describes a 12-month, phased plan to establish Dorina Salman as a recognized voice in mental health management — defined throughout this package as practical, non-clinical workplace wellbeing education: stress literacy, burnout prevention, boundaries, routines, emotional organization, and help-seeking behavior.

The plan converts Dorina's genuine, verifiable assets — 12+ years of executive administration experience, a business administration degree, Arabic/English bilingualism, and deep familiarity with Lebanese professional life — into a differentiated authority position that no clinical credential is required to hold, and that global health authorities explicitly endorse as necessary work.

The core thesis: The workplace mental health field has many clinicians and few translators. Dorina becomes the translator — the person who turns wellbeing guidance from authorities like the WHO into desk-level systems working people actually use. Authority is built through consistent LinkedIn education, converted into revenue through corporate workshops, and compounded through partnerships aligned with Lebanon's national mental health ecosystem.

Twelve-month outcome targets (defined fully in §8): - A rebuilt LinkedIn presence: from ~4 connections to 1,500+ relevant connections and 2,500+ followers. - 140+ published educational posts with a consistent voice and visual identity. - 8–12 workshops delivered (2–3 free pilots, then paid), with collected testimonials. - 3+ active partnerships (HR communities, companies, aligned organizations). - A flagship World Mental Health Day (October 10) campaign delivered. - A repeatable, approval-gated content engine that costs Dorina ~2–3 hours per week.

2. Why this, why now, why Dorina

2.1 The documented need

The World Health Organization's Mental health at work fact sheet establishes the scale of the problem this strategy addresses:

  • Almost 60% of the world population is in work.
  • 15% of working-age adults were estimated to have a mental disorder in 2019.
  • An estimated 12 billion working days are lost every year to depression and anxiety, costing US$1 trillion per year in lost productivity.
  • Workplace risks to mental health include excessive workloads and work pace, understaffing, long or inflexible hours, lack of control over one's work, poor physical conditions, and organizational culture problems.

Critically for this strategy, the WHO states that effective actions exist — to prevent risks, to protect and promote mental health at work, and to support workers — and its recommended actions include manager training, worker mental health literacy and awareness (to improve knowledge and reduce stigma), and interventions that build stress-management skills. These recommendations describe educational work. They are precisely the lane Dorina will occupy.

The WHO's Mental health: strengthening our response fact sheet adds the framing that gives the brand its moral center: mental health is a basic human right, exists on a continuum, and is shaped by individual, family, community, and structural factors. It also notes that the care gap for common conditions pushes countries toward innovative, non-specialist approaches — while treatment itself must remain in qualified pathways. Dorina's positioning sits exactly on the right side of that line: she widens literacy and prevention; she never touches treatment.

2.2 The Lebanese context

Lebanon has a National Mental Health Programme (NMHP) under the Ministry of Public Health, which has run national awareness campaigns, produced anti-stigma and psychological first aid materials, and — most relevantly — ran a "Mental Health in the Workplace" campaign under the slogan "Don't miss the opportunity / ما تضيعوا الفرصة", with partners including WHO, ABAAD, ACTED, ESA, the HR Association of Lebanon, IMC, Médecins du Monde, and SKOUN.

Three strategic implications:

  1. Legitimacy: Workplace mental health is nationally recognized as a priority in Lebanon. Dorina's work echoes an existing government campaign rather than importing a foreign idea.
  2. Partnership map: The NMHP's partner list is a ready-made map of organizations that already care about this topic (see OPPORTUNITY_MAP_LEBANON.md).
  3. Referral infrastructure: The NMHP's materials and pathways give Dorina credible, local places to point people who need real support — the backbone of her referral protocol.

2.4 The honest constraint — and why it is the strategy

No clinical license, psychology degree, or counseling credential is on file. Therefore Dorina will never be positioned as a therapist, psychologist, counselor, clinician, psychiatrist, trauma expert, or treatment provider. All materials define "mental health management" as self-management, workplace habits, stress literacy, boundaries, routines, and help-seeking — plus non-clinical workshops with clear referral protocols. This is stated in every deliverable and governed in RISK_ETHICS_AND_REFERRAL_PROTOCOL.md.

This constraint is a positioning gift: "the educator who knows her limits" is a more trustworthy brand than an ambiguous one, and it is the only durable one.

She is an educator and advocate, not a clinician: her work is literacy, prevention, and systems, with clear referral pathways to qualified professionals.

4. The signature framework: the DESK Method (proposed) (proposed)

Authority requires a teachable, ownable idea. We propose the DESK Method (proposed) — named for where working life actually happens, and for Dorina's administrative roots:

  • D — Detect. Stress literacy: learning to notice early personal and team signals of overload, using awareness-based education of the kind WHO recommends for improving knowledge and reducing stigma.
  • E — Establish. Boundaries, routines, and workload structures: the administrative craft of protecting time, energy, and attention — mapped against WHO-identified workplace risks such as excessive workload, long/inflexible hours, and lack of control.
  • S — Support. Help-seeking and peer support: normalizing asking for help, knowing what workplace support can and cannot do, and knowing the referral pathways to qualified professionals (aligned with Lebanon's NMHP resources).
  • K — Keep. Sustaining the habits: review rhythms, relapse-into-overwork prevention, and leisure/physical activity commitments — echoing WHO's recommendation of interventions for stress management and leisure-based physical activity.

Every workshop, content pillar, and product in this package hangs off this framework. It is deliberately non-clinical: it contains no assessment, no diagnosis, no therapeutic technique — only literacy, structure, and signposting.

5. The three-engine model

Engine 1 — Visibility (LinkedIn). Three educational posts weekly under four content pillars, an authority-grade profile, and deliberate network building toward HR professionals, office managers, team leads, and business owners in Lebanon and the region. Full system: LINKEDIN_AUTHORITY_ENGINE.md and 12_MONTH_LINKEDIN_CONTENT_CALENDAR.md.

Engine 2 — Revenue (Workshops). A four-workshop portfolio plus a 45-minute lunch-and-learn entry format, sold to companies and communities: literacy sessions, burnout-prevention systems, emotional organization for busy professionals, and a team-wellbeing session for managers (awareness-level, non-clinical, consistent with WHO's manager-training recommendation). Full system: WORKSHOP_SYSTEM_BLUEPRINT.md, WORKSHOP_EXERCISES_AND_HANDOUTS.md, pricing in MARKETING_FUNNEL_AND_CAMPAIGNS.md.

Engine 3 — Leverage (Partnerships & seasonal campaigns). Alliances with HR communities, universities, and organizations in the NMHP orbit; an annual flagship moment on World Mental Health Day, October 10; seasonal campaigns matched to the Lebanese work calendar. Full system: OPPORTUNITY_MAP_LEBANON.md, MARKETING_FUNNEL_AND_CAMPAIGNS.md.

6. Twelve-month phased roadmap

Assumed start: September 2026 (August 2026 = preparation). Dates shift cleanly if the start moves.

Phase 0 — Foundation (August 2026)

  • Dorina answers the "What we still need" questions; facts corrected (role, dates, third language).
  • Positioning title and DESK Method (proposed) name approved (or alternates chosen).
  • LinkedIn profile fully rewritten and photographed; banner and visual identity produced.
  • Approval workflow channel agreed; first month of content drafted and approved in advance.
  • Optional but recommended: enroll in a recognized non-clinical course (e.g., psychological first aid–type training within the WHO/NMHP ecosystem) to add a legitimate credential line.
  • Exit criteria: profile live, 12 posts pre-approved, referral protocol memorized.

Phase 1 — Presence (September–November 2026)

  • Posting cadence begins: 3 posts/week. Connection building: 15–25 targeted invitations/week with personal notes.
  • October flagship: World Mental Health Day (Oct 10) mini-campaign — a week of workplace mental health literacy content, culminating in an offer of a free lunch-and-learn to 3 companies.
  • Deliver 2–3 free pilot workshops (employer network, HR contacts) in exchange for written testimonials and photos.
  • Exit criteria: 400+ connections, 3 testimonials, one workshop fully rehearsed and timed.

Phase 2 — Proof (December 2026 – February 2027)

  • December theme: end-of-year fatigue and rest — seasonal, high-resonance content.
  • Convert pilots into the first paid bookings (introductory corporate pricing).
  • January "new work-year reset" campaign; first partnership conversations (HR communities, coworking spaces, universities).
  • Publish first long-form LinkedIn article: "What 12 years behind the executive desk taught me about burnout."
  • Exit criteria: 2+ paid workshops delivered, 800+ connections, 1 partnership in writing.

Phase 3 — Expansion (March–May 2027)

  • Raise workshop rates; introduce the manager-focused session.
  • Ramadan-aware content and scheduling (dates to be confirmed each year): energy, routine, and workload-compassion themes designed for the fasting month, delivered with cultural fluency.
  • Speak at or co-host one partner event; begin collecting a simple email list from workshop attendees (with consent).
  • Instagram extension launches once LinkedIn is stable (repurposing engine: INSTAGRAM_EXTENSION_ENGINE.md).
  • Exit criteria: 1,200+ connections, 6+ total workshops delivered, IG live with 2 posts/week repurposed.

Phase 4 — Authority (June–August 2027)

  • Summer series: rest, recovery, and sustainable pace (Lebanese summer/holiday season).
  • Package a half-day corporate program (two workshops + follow-up session) at premium pricing.
  • Begin planning the October 2027 World Mental Health Day campaign as an anchor event (possibly with a partner organization).
  • Review year-one metrics against §8; set year-two strategy (digital product, e.g., a paid "Emotional Organization" toolkit, only after live-delivery proof).
  • Exit criteria: 1,500+ connections, 8–12 workshops total, 3 partnerships, year-two plan approved.

10. Governance and ethical commitments

  1. Educational content only; never therapy, diagnosis, treatment, crisis support, or medical/legal/HR advice.
  2. Standing disclaimer on workshops and the profile; session-level safety language in every facilitation script.
  3. Crisis situations referred immediately to emergency services and qualified professionals; NMHP materials used as the local signpost.
  4. No invented statistics: all data cited from the four sources listed below; anything else is clearly framed as Dorina's experience or opinion.
  5. No content is published without Dorina's explicit approval of copy and graphic.
  6. Participant privacy: no photos, stories, or quotes used without consent.

Final Review Addendum — Preconditions Before Public Delivery

Before Dorina publishes the profile positioning or delivers paid workshops, complete these checks:

  • Confirm exact employment history and current employer restrictions; do not publish any dates or titles that Dorina has not verified.
  • Complete at least one non-clinical mental-health-literacy or Psychological First Aid training and one basic facilitation practice cycle before paid delivery.
  • Confirm local public/professional liability insurance options and obtain local legal review for disclaimers, privacy boundaries, workshop terms, and employer/moonlighting issues.
  • Verify current Lebanon crisis/referral contacts and operating hours before every printed handout or live workshop. Embrace Lebanon publicly lists 1564 for its Lifeline, but all details must be re-checked close to use.
  • Budget honestly: content drafting may take 2–3 hours/week, but the full engine including engagement, approvals, outreach, workshop preparation, delivery, and review can require 6–10+ hours/week during launch periods.
  • Track safeguarding metrics alongside growth: referral signposts made, boundary escalations, stopped exercises, post-workshop debrief notes, and whether the protocol was followed.
