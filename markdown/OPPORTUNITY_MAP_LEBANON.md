# Opportunity Map: Lebanon & Diaspora — Dorina Salman

**Purpose:** This document maps where, when, and through whom Dorina's authority as a **Workplace Wellbeing Educator | Mental Health Literacy Advocate** converts into real opportunities — paid workshops, partnerships, speaking slots, and retainer relationships — across the Lebanese market and the diaspora/GCC remote market. It is the "territory" companion to the "vehicle" documents: the offers live in `WORKSHOP_SYSTEM_BLUEPRINT.md`, the conversion machinery in `MARKETING_FUNNEL_AND_CAMPAIGNS.md`, the visibility engine in `12_MONTH_LINKEDIN_CONTENT_CALENDAR.md`, and the guardrails in `RISK_ETHICS_AND_REFERRAL_PROTOCOL.md`. Everything here is strictly non-clinical: education on stress literacy, burnout prevention, boundaries, routines, workload structure, and help-seeking — never therapy, diagnosis, or treatment.

> Educational content on workplace wellbeing — not therapy, diagnosis, or crisis support. If you're struggling, please reach a qualified professional; in an emergency, contact local emergency services.

---

## 1. How to read this map

An **opportunity** in this strategy is not a sector or a contact — it is an intersection of four things:

> **Opportunity = Audience pain × Access route × Seasonal moment × Offer fit**

- **Audience pain** — a workplace stressor the audience already feels and names (deadlines, workload, long hours, unclear roles). The WHO's list of workplace risks to mental health — excessive workloads and work pace, understaffing, long or inflexible hours, lack of control over job design, under-use of skills, poor physical conditions, and negative workplace culture — is our anchor vocabulary. If a sector's daily reality maps onto that list, wellbeing education resonates there.
- **Access route** — a realistic path to the decision-maker: a warm contact, a community, an alumni network, a public event. No access route, no opportunity — however large the sector.
- **Seasonal moment** — the month when the door is naturally open (World Mental Health Day on 10 October, January reset, pre-summer fatigue). Section 3 maps these.
- **Offer fit** — the right first workshop from the W0–W5 portfolio in `WORKSHOP_SYSTEM_BLUEPRINT.md`. Almost every sector enters through **W0 (free lunch & learn taster)** because it removes budget risk; the sector map notes where a different entry makes sense.

### Scoring rubric (used consistently in Section 2)

| Dimension | Question it answers | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Access** | Can Dorina realistically reach the decision-maker in the next 6 months? | No warm path; cold outreach only | One-step introduction plausible (community, alumni, ex-colleague) | Existing relationship or insider status |
| **Fit** | Does the DESK Method (proposed) and non-clinical positioning match what this buyer needs? | Buyer likely wants clinical services or something else | Fit with adaptation | Direct fit; her story is native to this world |
| **Effort** | How much preparation, customization, and logistics per engagement? | High effort (procurement, long cycles, heavy tailoring) | Moderate | Light (standard workshop lands as-is) |
| **Revenue potential** | Realistic 12-month paid ceiling, assuming pilots convert per `MARKETING_FUNNEL_AND_CAMPAIGNS.md`? | Free/goodwill mostly | Occasional paid W1–W3 | Repeat business, W5 retainer plausible |

**Reading rule:** a sector with High Access and High Fit but Low Revenue (e.g., some nonprofit/humanitarian organization or community work) is still valuable — it builds proof, testimonials, and referrals that unlock High-Revenue sectors later. In year one, **Access beats Revenue** as the sorting key. Note that on the Effort row, "High" effort is a *cost*, not a virtue — read High-Effort sectors as slower bets.

## 2. Sector map

Ten sectors, each assessed against the rubric. All Lebanon-market specifics (which firms, how many, what they pay) are deliberately absent — they are **to verify** during the 90-day shortlist in Section 7. Sector *types* below are suggestions to explore, not asserted market facts.

### 2a. Law firms & professional services — her home turf

- **Why wellbeing education resonates:** legal and professional-services work concentrates several WHO-listed risks in one place: excessive workloads and pace, long and inflexible hours, and a culture where admitting strain can feel career-limiting. Dorina spent 12+ years inside a law-firm environment as an executive assistant — she does not need to imagine this pain; she administered around it daily.
- **Realistic entry point:** direct, personal outreach to former colleagues, office managers, and partners she worked with or alongside. This is the only sector where she can open with "you know my work."
- **First workshop:** **W0** taster for the whole office, positioned as "workload, energy, and focus for high-pressure teams," converting to **W1** (flagship half-day) or **W3** (admin-professionals session for the support staff she once was).
- **Decision-maker:** managing partner or office manager/practice manager; in larger firms, an HR lead (to verify per firm).
- **Obstacles:** billable-hours culture makes half-days hard to schedule (offer W2's 2-hour format as fallback); confidentiality-minded firms may hesitate to discuss "mental health" openly (lead with the workload/focus framing).
- **Scores:** Access **High** · Fit **High** · Effort **High (light lift)** · Revenue **Medium**

### 2b. Marketing, creative & media agencies — her current world

- **Why it resonates:** deadline culture, client-driven pace, always-on communication, and understaffed teams — a near-verbatim match to the WHO workplace risk list (excessive pace, long hours, lack of control over deliverables). Burnout language is already normalized in agency talk, lowering stigma barriers.
- **Realistic entry point:** her current employer (see the constraint in Section 6 — employer blessing first), then the agency's client and peer network, then agency-community events (to verify locally).
- **First workshop:** **W0** internally as a rehearsal-with-witnesses; then **W2** (manager session) sold to agency leadership — agency managers are young, promoted fast, and rarely trained to spot team strain, which is exactly what WHO recommends manager training for.
- **Decision-maker:** founder/managing director in smaller agencies; HR or operations lead in larger ones (to verify).
- **Obstacles:** conflict-of-interest optics with her employer (resolve in writing before any external agency outreach); agencies are cost-sensitive — keep pilots free or modular per `MARKETING_FUNNEL_AND_CAMPAIGNS.md`.
- **Scores:** Access **High** · Fit **High** · Effort **High (light lift)** · Revenue **Medium**

### 2c. Banks & financial institutions

- **Why it resonates:** high-pressure targets, compliance load, long hours, and — as a planning assumption — years of sector stress in Lebanon's financial context have left workforces fatigued. WHO's recommended organizational actions (manager training, mental-health literacy, stress-management skills) map directly onto what an HR department in this sector could commission.
- **Realistic entry point:** HR and training departments, reached via HR communities (Section 4, Tier 1) rather than cold-calling branches. Banks buy from vendors with references — so this is a **year-one-seeding, year-two-selling** sector.
- **First workshop:** **W0** for one department as a pilot, explicitly framed as literacy/education; **W5** quarterly retainer is the long-term prize, since banks prefer structured recurring programs.
- **Decision-maker:** HR director or learning & development manager (to verify per institution).
- **Obstacles:** procurement processes, vendor-registration requirements, and preference for established training houses; slow decision cycles.
- **Scores:** Access **Low** · Fit **Medium** · Effort **Low (heavy lift)** · Revenue **High**

### 2d. nonprofit/humanitarian organizations & humanitarian/development organizations

- **Why it resonates:** humanitarian and development work is structurally high-stress — emergency tempo, funding uncertainty, exposure to others' distress, understaffing. Critically, this sector is *adjacent to the national ecosystem*: Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health runs a National Mental Health Programme (NMHP) which has run a national "Mental Health in the Workplace" campaign ("Don't miss the opportunity / ما تضيعوا الفرصة") with publicly listed partners including WHO, ABAAD, ACTED, ESA, the HR Association of Lebanon, IMC, Médecins du Monde, and SKOUN. That public list is a map of organizations that already care about workplace mental health. Dorina claims **no affiliation** with any of them — but their existence proves the conversation is nationally live.
- **Realistic entry point:** HR/staff-care focal points in nonprofit/humanitarian organizations, reached through professional networks and public sector events; offering *staff wellbeing education* (for their own teams), not program delivery to beneficiaries.
- **First workshop:** **W0** for an nonprofit/humanitarian organization's admin and operations staff; **W4** (online 90-min) works well for nonprofit/humanitarian organizations with field-distributed teams.
- **Decision-maker:** HR manager, staff-care/duty-of-care focal point, or country operations lead (titles vary — to verify).
- **Obstacles:** many nonprofit/humanitarian organizations have in-house psychosocial expertise and will scrutinize scope — which is actually an asset: her clearly non-clinical, education-only positioning (with `RISK_ETHICS_AND_REFERRAL_PROTOCOL.md` shareable on request) is exactly what a careful nonprofit/humanitarian organization wants to see. Budgets may favor free/low-cost engagements initially.
- **Scores:** Access **Medium** · Fit **High** · Effort **Medium** · Revenue **Low–Medium** (but High referral and credibility value)

### 2e. Hospitals & clinics — administrative and non-clinical staff ONLY

- **Scope statement, explicit:** Dorina serves the **administrative workforce** of healthcare institutions — receptionists, billing and records staff, schedulers, office managers, HR teams. She does **not** train, advise, or support clinical staff on clinical matters, and she does not deliver anything resembling clinical supervision or staff counseling. This boundary goes in writing in every healthcare proposal.
- **Why it resonates:** hospital admin staff absorb the pressure of the clinical environment — pace, understaffing, emotionally charged interactions at the front desk — without the professional support structures clinicians may have. They are her tribe wearing a different badge.
- **Realistic entry point:** hospital HR/administration departments, framed precisely: "workplace wellbeing education for your non-clinical teams."
- **First workshop:** **W3** (admin-professionals session) is the natural first offer here — more specific than W0 and instantly signals she understands this workforce.
- **Decision-maker:** HR director or director of administration (to verify per institution).
- **Obstacles:** healthcare institutions are highly sensitive to scope-of-practice questions — any ambiguity about "mental health" services will stall the deal. Lead every conversation with the non-clinical scope statement and the standing disclaimer.
- **Scores:** Access **Low–Medium** · Fit **High (for admin staff)** · Effort **Medium** · Revenue **Medium**

### 2f. Universities & schools — staff wellbeing + the AOU network

- **Why it resonates:** administrative and academic-support staff in education face term-time surges, exam-season pressure, and under-recognized workloads — WHO's "under-use of skills" and workload-spike risks in institutional form. Separately, universities host exactly the audience (young professionals and soon-to-be professionals) her LinkedIn content serves.
- **Realistic entry point:** **Arab Open University – Lebanon alumni relations** — Dorina is a B.B.A. graduate and this is a real network she can activate: alumni newsletters, guest-talk slots, career-services events. Other universities and schools follow once she has an AOU talk as proof.
- **First workshop:** a **guest talk** (adapted W0 content) for alumni or staff; **W1** for administrative staff development days; **W4** for alumni abroad.
- **Decision-maker:** alumni-relations office, HR/staff-development office, or student-affairs teams for career-adjacent talks (to verify).
- **Obstacles:** academic calendars book far ahead (approach in September for winter/spring slots); talks are usually unpaid — treat them as authority assets feeding the funnel in `MARKETING_FUNNEL_AND_CAMPAIGNS.md`.
- **Scores:** Access **High (AOU) / Medium (others)** · Fit **High** · Effort **High (light lift)** · Revenue **Low** (High visibility value)

### 2g. Tech startups & remote-first companies

- **Why it resonates:** startup culture normalizes overwork; remote work blurs boundaries between work and rest — directly the WHO risks of excessive pace, long/inflexible hours, and culture problems. Founders are often receptive to "sustainable performance" framing, and small headcounts mean one yes from one founder is a full engagement.
- **Realistic entry point:** startup community events, coworking-space programming (see 2i), and LinkedIn itself — this audience actually reads it. Explore local startup hubs and entrepreneurship-support organizations as venue categories (specific names to verify).
- **First workshop:** **W4** (online 90-min) fits remote-first teams natively; **W0** in-person for co-located startups.
- **Decision-maker:** founder or head of people/operations — often the same person.
- **Obstacles:** cash-sensitive; churn (a startup client may not exist next year); preference for free content. Counter with low-priced W4 and community bundles.
- **Scores:** Access **Medium** · Fit **High** · Effort **High (light lift)** · Revenue **Medium**

### 2h. SMEs & family businesses

- **Why it resonates:** in small and family-run firms there is no HR department to catch strain — the owner is HR. Understaffing and role overload (WHO-listed risks) are structural: everyone does three jobs. A practical, jargon-free session on workload structure and boundaries speaks their language, especially when framed as *keeping good people* rather than "mental health."
- **Realistic entry point:** business networks, chambers-of-commerce-type bodies, and owner communities (categories to explore, names to verify); referrals from her law-firm network, since law firms serve SME owners.
- **First workshop:** **W2** adapted for owners ("how to spot strain in your team before people quit"), or **W0** at a business-community event reaching many owners at once.
- **Decision-maker:** the owner. One conversation, one decision — the shortest sales cycle on this map.
- **Obstacles:** "wellbeing" can sound like a luxury to a stretched owner; extreme price sensitivity as a planning assumption. Lead with retention and productivity framing (WHO: an estimated 12 billion working days are lost every year to depression and anxiety, at about US$1 trillion in lost productivity — the global-scale version of the owner's local problem).
- **Scores:** Access **Medium** · Fit **Medium–High** · Effort **High (light lift)** · Revenue **Medium**

### 2i. Coworking spaces, business communities & professional syndicates/associations

- **Why it resonates:** these are not end-buyers so much as **multipliers** — one session in a coworking space or association event puts Dorina in front of dozens of decision-makers from sectors 2a–2h simultaneously. Community managers constantly need quality free programming; she constantly needs stages. The trade is natural.
- **Realistic entry point:** direct proposal to community/events managers: "a free 60-minute lunch & learn on workload, energy, and focus for your members." Professional associations (HR bodies, admin-professional groups, sector syndicates — categories to verify) work the same way at higher credibility.
- **First workshop:** **W0**, always — it is literally built for this. Each event feeds email capture and follow-up per `MARKETING_FUNNEL_AND_CAMPAIGNS.md`.
- **Decision-maker:** community manager, events coordinator, or association board/committee member.
- **Obstacles:** free-programming expectations (fine in year one — this is the seeding channel); crowded event calendars around popular dates — book the October World Mental Health Day window early.
- **Scores:** Access **High** · Fit **High** · Effort **High (light lift)** · Revenue **Low direct / High indirect**

### 2j. Diaspora & GCC remote market — W4 online

- **Why it resonates:** as a planning assumption, Lebanon has a significant diaspora, including many professionals in GCC countries, often working demanding schedules far from family support systems. They share her language, cultural context, and workplace idioms; a bilingual Arabic/English educator who *gets* both the Lebanese office and the expat grind is a differentiated offer. GCC-based employers and Lebanese-owned businesses abroad also budget in stronger currencies — a pricing advantage.
- **Realistic entry point:** LinkedIn (the diaspora professional's default network), diaspora professional groups and alumni chapters (AOU alumni abroad — to verify what chapters exist), and referrals from Lebanon-based clients whose sister companies operate in the GCC.
- **First workshop:** **W4** (online 90-min) exclusively at first — it was designed for this market. Time-zone friendliness with the GCC is workable from Lebanon.
- **Decision-maker:** individual professionals (paid tickets or free-then-nurture), community organizers, and eventually GCC-based HR contacts.
- **Obstacles:** zero physical presence; connectivity on Dorina's side must be redundant (see Section 6); building trust remotely takes longer — the `12_MONTH_LINKEDIN_CONTENT_CALENDAR.md` is the primary trust engine here.
- **Scores:** Access **Medium** · Fit **High** · Effort **Medium** · Revenue **Medium–High over time**

## 4. Partnership & ecosystem map

Five tiers, ordered by how directly they convert. For every tier: what Dorina gives, what she gets, the first-touch move, and the risk to avoid. **Standing rule for all tiers:** Dorina never claims affiliation, partnership, or endorsement by any named organization. Named organizations below are ecosystem actors and *potential* partners to approach respectfully.

### Tier 1 — Professional communities (HR-focused associations and communities)

- **Why this tier leads:** HR professionals are the buyers. Notably, the HR Association of Lebanon appears publicly on the MoPH page as a partner of the NMHP's national "Mental Health in the Workplace" campaign — which signals that HR communities in Lebanon are already engaged with this exact topic. That makes HR communities a natural ally *category* (approach respectfully; no affiliation claims; specific communities and their current activity to verify).
- **Give:** free, high-quality W0 content for their member events; a bilingual speaker who shows up prepared; co-created literacy content their members can use.
- **Get:** direct access to a room full of buyers; credibility by association with professional bodies; recurring speaking slots.
- **First-touch move:** a short, respectful message to the community's events lead: "I deliver a free 60-minute educational session on workplace wellbeing for professional communities — non-clinical, practical, bilingual. Could this fit one of your upcoming member events?"
- **Risk to avoid:** implying the association endorses her or that she is affiliated with any national campaign. She is an independent educator offering member value, full stop.

### Tier 2 — NMHP-ecosystem nonprofit/humanitarian organizations

- **Context:** the Ministry of Public Health's National Mental Health Programme publishes awareness campaigns, anti-stigma material, and psychological first aid material, and its workplace campaign's partners are listed publicly (including ABAAD, ACTED, ESA, IMC, Médecins du Monde, SKOUN, alongside WHO and the HR Association of Lebanon). That public list is intelligence: it shows which organizations have invested in workplace mental health in Lebanon.
- **Give:** what a non-clinical educator can legitimately offer such organizations — volunteer workplace-literacy content (e.g., plain-language explainers for their staff-facing channels), facilitation of explicitly *non-clinical* segments at their events (logistics-savvy, bilingual, reliable), and amplification of their public anti-stigma materials to her audience.
- **Get:** ecosystem legitimacy; learning from organizations that navigate this space professionally; referral pathways for her own referral protocol (see `RISK_ETHICS_AND_REFERRAL_PROTOCOL.md`); possible future invitations to workplace-focused initiatives.
- **First-touch move:** attend their public events first; engage genuinely with their published campaigns on LinkedIn; only then send a modest offer of volunteer support in her defined lane. Ecosystem relationships are earned slowly.
- **Risk to avoid:** two failure modes — (1) overclaiming: presenting herself as connected to the NMHP or any partner; (2) scope drift: accepting a role in anything clinical or crisis-adjacent because an nonprofit/humanitarian organization asked. Both are declined per Section 8.

### Tier 3 — Business enablers (chambers, business networks, coworking spaces)

- **Give:** free member programming (W0), a professional-development angle that differentiates the enabler's event calendar, and cross-promotion to her growing audience.
- **Get:** stages, SME-owner and startup-founder audiences, venue partnerships (a coworking space hosting her paid W1 in exchange for member discounts — structure to negotiate), and local business credibility.
- **First-touch move:** identify three business-community venues (categories: coworking spaces, business networks, chamber-type bodies — specific names to verify), visit in person where possible, and propose a free October session tied to World Mental Health Day.
- **Risk to avoid:** becoming permanent free entertainment. After two free sessions with any single enabler, the conversation moves to paid formats or venue-partnership terms per `MARKETING_FUNNEL_AND_CAMPAIGNS.md`.

### Tier 4 — Academia (AOU alumni relations and guest talks)

- **Give:** an alumna success story the university can showcase; free guest talks for students and alumni on career-relevant wellbeing (workload, boundaries, sustainable early-career habits); willingness to appear in alumni communications.
- **Get:** an institutional stage with built-in trust; access to the AOU alumni network (her one *real*, activatable institutional network); photos and recordings that become authority assets; young-professional audience growth.
- **First-touch move:** email the AOU – Lebanon alumni-relations office as a graduate: brief bio, the positioning line, and an offer of a free talk for an alumni or student event — proposing the October window or a January "new year, sustainable habits" angle.
- **Risk to avoid:** letting talks drift toward student mental-health support, which is a wellbeing-services domain owned by professionals. Her talks are about *workplace* skills and habits; anything personal gets the referral protocol response.

### Tier 5 — Media (podcasts, business press wellbeing columns)

- **Give:** an articulate, bilingual guest with a fresh angle — the executive assistant who became a workplace-wellbeing educator — and ready-made, evidence-anchored talking points (the WHO working-days-lost figure lands well in business media).
- **Get:** borrowed audiences, authority-by-platform, quotable assets for the LinkedIn calendar, and SEO-relevant mentions.
- **First-touch move:** list regional business podcasts and workplace/HR-adjacent media (specific outlets to verify); pitch a tight, specific episode idea ("what admin professionals see about burnout that managers miss") rather than a generic "have me on."
- **Risk to avoid:** being framed as a mental-health *expert* in the clinical sense. She supplies her own framing in every pitch — "workplace wellbeing educator, non-clinical" — and gently corrects introductions that overstate it.

Concrete play: every proposal states "delivered in Arabic, English, or both — your choice," a line most training vendors cannot honestly write.

Even at the exposure level, she absorbs how professional content gets made, which compounds the `12_MONTH_LINKEDIN_CONTENT_CALENDAR.md` execution. Concrete play: production polish on workshop materials and LinkedIn assets that outclasses typical solo trainers.
4. **The law-firm network.** Lawyers and their clients form a referral web that touches SMEs (2h), banks' legal departments (2c), and other professional-services firms (2a). Concrete play: her first three W0 sessions and her first paid W1 most plausibly come from this network; every law-firm session ends with "who else should hear this?"
5. **From-zero authenticity as a story asset.** Rebuilding public presence from near zero is not a weakness to hide — it is the narrative spine of the LinkedIn calendar: a mid-career professional building expertise in public, documenting the journey honestly. Audiences trust builders more than arrivers. Concrete play: the "building in public" thread makes early small wins (first talk, first workshop, first testimonial) into content, converting progress itself into authority.

## 8. Opportunity qualification checklist

Before saying **yes** to any engagement — paid or free — run this checklist. A single hard "no" on items 1–3 means decline or renegotiate.

1. **Audience fit.** Is the room made of working professionals attending in a workplace/professional-development context? (Not: patients, therapy-seeking groups, minors, or clinical populations.)
2. **Non-clinical scope confirmed in writing.** Does the written agreement/email trail describe the session as *education on workplace wellbeing* — and has the host seen the standing disclaimer? If the host's promotional copy says "therapy," "counseling," "healing," or "mental health support session," it gets corrected before Dorina appears under it.
3. **Referral protocol shareable.** Can she share `RISK_ETHICS_AND_REFERRAL_PROTOCOL.md` (or its client-facing summary) with the host, including the helpline placeholder — [TO VERIFY before use: current national emotional-support helpline number — check the Ministry of Public Health / National Mental Health Programme site] — and is the host comfortable with it? A host who resists the safety framing is a red flag.
4. **Decision-maker engaged.** Is the person who can approve payment, scheduling, and follow-on work in the conversation — not only an enthusiastic intermediary?
5. **Logistics feasible.** Venue, timing outside employment conflicts, power/connectivity fallback for the format, room setup, and (for W4) platform tested? Per `WORKSHOP_SYSTEM_BLUEPRINT.md` checklists.
6. **Strategic value if unpaid.** If free: does it deliver at least two of — a target-sector audience, a testimonial opportunity, content assets, a referral path? Free work with none of these is politely declined; her calendar is the scarcest asset she owns.
7. **Story safety.** Nothing about the engagement pressures her to present as a clinician, share attendee disclosures, or handle personal crises in the room.

### Graceful-decline script (out-of-scope requests)

For requests that drift clinical — "can you run a group therapy-style session," "our employee is struggling, can you talk to her," "can you assess our team's mental health":

> "Thank you for trusting me with this — it tells me you genuinely care about your people. What you're describing needs a qualified mental-health professional, and that's outside my scope: I'm an educator, not a clinician, and your team deserves the right kind of support. What I *can* do is [the in-scope alternative: a workshop on stress literacy and workload structure / a session that teaches teams how and when to seek help]. And I can point you toward finding qualified professional support — my referral protocol covers exactly this."

Then follow `RISK_ETHICS_AND_REFERRAL_PROTOCOL.md`: refer, don't rescue; document the exchange; never name-drop specific clinicians as "partners" without an actual agreement. Arabic soft opener where useful: **"هيدا الموضوع بيستاهل شخص مختصّ"** — *"this matter deserves a specialist"* — which declines while honoring the request.
