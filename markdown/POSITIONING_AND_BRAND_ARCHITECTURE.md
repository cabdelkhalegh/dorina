## 1. The niche

**Category:** Workplace wellbeing education & mental health literacy (non-clinical).
**Micro-niche (the wedge):** *Emotional organization* — applying the craft of administrative systems (calendars, boundaries, workload structure, communication hygiene) to how working people manage stress and energy.

Why this wedge wins:

1. **It is unoccupied.** Wellbeing voices are mostly clinicians (who can't speak "office operations") or motivational speakers (who have no systems). An administrator who systematizes wellbeing is a genuinely new archetype.
2. **It is credible without a clinical license.** Nothing in "how to structure your workload, protect your boundaries, and know when and where to seek help" requires — or implies — clinical authority.
3. **It is WHO-aligned.** The WHO's recommended workplace actions include worker mental health literacy and awareness (knowledge + stigma reduction) and stress-management skills interventions. The WHO also names concrete workplace risks — excessive workloads and pace, understaffing, long/inflexible hours, lack of control — that are *organizational and administrative* in nature. Dorina teaches at exactly that intersection.
4. **It is nationally resonant.** Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health has run a national "Mental Health in the Workplace" campaign ("Don't miss the opportunity / ما تضيعوا الفرصة"). The topic has local legitimacy and an existing partner ecosystem.

**One-line niche statement:**
> Practical mental health management for working professionals — taught as systems, not slogans.

## 3. Audience architecture

### Primary audiences (content + buyers)

1. **HR professionals & people managers in Lebanon** — the *buyers*. They need affordable, safe, credible wellbeing programming; a non-clinical educator is easier to book than a clinical service. WHO's manager-training recommendation gives them a mandate.
2. **Administrative professionals, executive assistants, office managers** — the *tribe*. Dorina is their voice: overworked, invisible, holding companies together. High identification, high sharing, high loyalty.
3. **Young professionals & team leads (25–40) in Lebanese SMEs, agencies, law firms, and startups** — the *audience*. They live the WHO risk list daily (workload, hours, lack of control) and want practical tools, not lectures.

### Secondary audiences

4. **Business owners of Lebanese SMEs** — buy workshops when framed as productivity and retention (WHO's US$1 trillion productivity-loss figure is the door-opener).
5. **Lebanese diaspora & regional (GCC) professionals** — extend reach and future online-workshop demand; English content serves them.
6. **nonprofit/humanitarian organization / development-sector staff in Lebanon** — high-stress sector, workshop demand, and adjacency to the NMHP partner ecosystem.

### Explicitly *not* the audience
People seeking help for mental disorders, therapy alternatives, or crisis support. Content will occasionally be seen by them; every asset therefore carries referral signposting (see §8).

## 5. Signature framework: the DESK Method (proposed)

The ownable intellectual property. One page Dorina can draw on a whiteboard in 60 seconds:

| Letter | Name | What it teaches | WHO/NMHP grounding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D** | **Detect** | Stress literacy: noticing early personal and team overload signals; naming stress without stigma | WHO: literacy/awareness improves knowledge and reduces stigma |
| **E** | **Establish** | Boundaries, routines, workload visibility, communication hygiene | WHO risk list: workload, pace, hours, control — all addressable by structure |
| **S** | **Support** | Help-seeking as a skill; what workplace support is (and is not); referral pathways | WHO: support for workers; NMHP materials as local signposts |
| **K** | **Keep** | Sustaining habits: weekly reviews, rest as a system, leisure and physical activity | WHO: stress-management and leisure/physical-activity interventions |

**Naming alternatives if Dorina prefers:** "The Organized Mind Method," "WorkWell Systems," "الترتيب النفسي (Emotional Organization) Framework." The structure stays; the label is hers to choose.  is aspirational branding, not a registered mark — registration can be explored later.

## 9. Verbal identity

- **Tone:** warm, structured, direct; "an organized friend who has seen real offices." Confident about systems, humble about science: statistics are attributed ("According to the WHO…"), lived experience is owned ("In twelve years behind the desk…").
- **Vocabulary to use:** wellbeing, stress literacy, burnout prevention, boundaries, routines, workload, energy, help-seeking, psychological safety (as a workplace-culture concept), mental health literacy, emotional organization.
- **Vocabulary to avoid:** diagnose, treat, heal, therapy/therapeutic (about her own work), patients, disorders (except when citing WHO data), trauma work, "fix your anxiety/depression," any promise of clinical outcomes.
- **Bilingual principle:** English for regional/professional reach; Arabic for resonance and stigma-reduction at home. High-emotion advocacy posts land hardest in Arabic; systems posts work well in English. Key posts can run bilingually (Arabic first).

## 10. Visual identity direction (brief for design)

- **Feel:** calm authority — closer to a premium consultancy than a wellness aesthetic. No lotus flowers, no head-in-hands stock photos (stigma-reinforcing), no medical imagery (boundary-blurring).
- **Palette direction:** a grounded base (deep teal or navy) + a warm accent (sand/amber) + generous white space. Final palette chosen with Dorina's preference (open question #7).
- **Typography:** clean humanist sans-serif; genuinely bilingual — Arabic type treated as a first-class citizen, not an afterthought.
- **Recurring assets:** LinkedIn banner, post/carousel templates per pillar, quote cards, workshop slide master, one-page handout template. Production briefs and prompts: `PREMIUM_PROMPT_LIBRARY.md` §G.
